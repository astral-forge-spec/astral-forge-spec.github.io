Research

Overview

My research investigates how to formally evaluate the quality of approximation algorithms. My recent work applied an information-theoretic metric to locality-sensitive hashing for sequence alignment. I am broadly interested in the complexity-theoretic limits of approximation and in theories of algorithms that reason under uncertainty and strategic interaction, particularly computational learning theory and algorithmic game theory.

Projects

Information-Theoretic Evaluation and Design of Locality-Sensitive Hashing for Protein Alignment

May 2026

Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH) is a technique of algorithms used to approximate edit distances. This research focused on constructing a metric to measure the performance of these algorithms, as well as developing a new algorithm.

Conducted under SURA

Generalizing Tiered Trees to Hypertrees

Jul 2024

A tiered tree is a tree with a tiering function. We generalized this concept into hypergraphs and hypertrees and designed and implemented a C library to compute hypertree weights and to extend the Tutte polynomial to hypertrees.

Conducted under PROMYS

Theoretical Approach on Assessing the Accuracy of the Shortest Path Non-Optimal Algorithm for 2-Dimensional Grids with Obstacles

Mar 2022

Traditional search algorithms on 2D grids guarantee to find the shortest path at the cost of quadratic time complexity. Non-optimal algorithms that trade accuracy for speed have emerged to address the issue. This research evaluates the accuracy of two non-optimal algorithms using experimental data and theoretical analysis.

Published at ICACS '24
